# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Library

- `pdf::render` draws footnotes and endnotes. Each page's footnotes sit at its foot under the separator, from the footnotes part's separator story: Word's default is a 2-inch rule at the paragraph's indent, 0.48 pt thick, its top 0.61 of its line's height below the line's top (Word for Mac 16.115's PDFs of the corpus and the long note, within 0.6 pt). The continuation separator above a note going on from the page before spans the line. Separators are drawn as their own paragraphs, in their run's colour; where the separator's paragraph spacing counts, it is above the line (`layout --lines` moves the separator's line down by it). Notes are drawn in their own fonts, the font chain deciding their text and marks, with their links, bookmarks and pictures; endnotes come after the last block under the endnote separator. A page's text extracts as its body, then its notes. A note holding a table that would go on to the next page is refused with `pdf_footnote_layout_unsupported`.
- `pdf::preflight` no longer reports footnotes and endnotes as not yet drawn, and now looks into the separator stories too. Notes beneath the text, footnote columns and endnotes at the ends of several sections are still `pdf_footnote_layout_unsupported`.
- `pdf::render` draws pictures (DrawingML `wp:inline` and `wp:anchor`), in the body, headers, footers and notes (each reading its own part's relationships), each media part written once as an image XObject however often it is shown. An inline picture sits on its line's baseline at its extent, inside its effect extent, with its crop (`a:srcRect`, clipped to the extent), rotation and flips. An anchored picture is placed by `positionH`/`positionV` (or `simplePos`) against the page, margin, column, character, paragraph, line or a margin area, inside and outside margins by the parity of the number the page shows, by offset, percentage (`wp14:pctPos*Offset`) or alignment; it takes no part in text flow. A page draws its headers' and footers' layer under the body's, each with its behind-text pictures, its text, then its in-front pictures, by `relativeHeight`; the anchored pictures' layers count toward the content cap (`max_pdf_content_bytes`). What leaves the page, a full-page cover background or an oversized footer picture, is cut by the page's edge. A picture that cannot be drawn (an unsupported or damaged format, or a part the package does not hold) is a grey box with a cross and `pdf_image_unsupported`; an image's colour profile or animation found while drawing is added to the report (`pdf_image_profile_ignored`, `pdf_image_animated`), and `strict` refuses it. Pixel budgets apply as before.
- `pdf::preflight` no longer reports DrawingML pictures as not drawn yet. A picture whose relationship names no part is `pdf_image_unsupported` (`missing image`), and a chart or SmartArt read through a picture fallback (`mc:AlternateContent` whose supported choice is its `mc:Fallback`) is the new `pdf_drawing_fallback_used`. VML pictures stay `pdf_not_yet_drawn`.
- Page estimates read a run's `mc:AlternateContent` through its first supported choice, and an inline object's box includes its `wp:effectExtent`. A line holding a picture is as tall as Word for Mac makes it (the corpus's showcase pictures): the picture's bottom on the baseline, with the line's text descent and multiple-spacing extra below it, or, for a picture alone on its line, the extra only. The 21 showcase conversions' snapshots moved. 21 of them have their pictures on Word's page, and in 20 every line after a picture is within 0.3 pt of Word's baseline (up to 10.7 pt before); the exception is `compat-word2003-legacy`, where a line break of Word's compatibility mode 11 moves the lines after it. 2,630 of the corpus's 2,753 compared paragraphs are on Word's page (2,626 before).
- At the top of a page that a page break starts, a paragraph keeps its space before less the previous paragraph's space after (never below 0), as Word for Mac does. This applies after a next-page section break in every document (estimates kept the whole space before), and after `pageBreakBefore` only when the document's compatibility mode is below 15 or missing (estimates kept none; in mode 15 they still do). After an explicit page break, or where contextual spacing removes both spaces, the paragraph keeps none. The TOC fixtures' page-2 heading is 19 pt lower (24 pt before, 5 pt after), and page estimates now put all 252 compared paragraphs of the PDF fixtures on Word's page (247 before).
- Experimental `pdf` feature.
- `pdf::render` makes links clickable and gives the PDF an outline. Each hyperlink (`w:hyperlink` or a `HYPERLINK` field) is a `/Link` annotation per line it is on, as Word for Mac draws them: from the line's top to its bottom, and on the paragraph's last line down through its space after, from 2.16 pt before its text to 2.16 pt after its last drawn character, or past the right edge of the text when it goes on onto the next line, the spaces between its words and their justified widening included. Each bookmark is a named destination at its line's top, 2.16 pt left of where the line's text starts, as Word writes them, so contents entries go to their headings. Each body paragraph with an outline level through its style chain (`w:outlineLvl`; the built-in `heading 1` to `heading 9` styles without one take theirs) is an outline item at its first line, titled with its list label and text. `PdfOptions::outline` and `PdfOptions::links` (both on by default) leave them out. A hyperlink relationship or `HYPERLINK` field to `#name` goes to the bookmark `name`. A bookmark outside a paragraph (between blocks, before a table or between its rows, or in deleted text) goes to the start of the next paragraph, for links and for page numbers. A link the navigation leaves out that the preflight did not count is added to the report. Links and bookmarks in table cells and in footnotes and endnotes are placed as in the body; a heading in a table cell is an outline item, one in a note is not, and a heading's note reference is left out of its title. Headers and footers have no links until they are drawn.
- `pdf::preflight` no longer reports hyperlinks and `HYPERLINK` fields as not yet drawn. A link on a picture or shape is still reported, as `picture link`.
- `pdf::render` draws each page's header and footer (first, even or default) and computes PAGE, NUMPAGES, SECTIONPAGES and PAGEREF, the contents' page numbers included, from the PDF's own pages; the document is unchanged. Only the headers and footers some page shows are checked for fonts and reported: the font chain decides their text, and every character a page field's value can show in its format, and refuses with the body. What layout changes is reported with its story and page, and `strict` refuses it: a header or footer cut off at the middle of the page (`pdf_story_overflow`); a page number written in arabic for a format layout does not write, a section's included (words, ordinals, chapter numbers) (`pdf_field_format_approximated`); an ignored `\p` switch (`pdf_field_switch_ignored`); a page reference to a bookmark layout does not place (`pdf_field_cached`). `preflight` reads a field as layout does: PAGE, NUMPAGES, SECTIONPAGES and PAGEREF it computes, and TOC, are no longer reported; one it does not compute (`PAGE extra`, a bare `PAGEREF`), and DATE, TIME and PRINTDATE, are `pdf_field_cached`; so are a contents entry whose number is plain text rather than a PAGEREF field, or plain text and a PAGEREF field together, and a field without an end, each with a message of its own. Chapter numbers are reported for a page number in any format, `\* Arabic` included, by `preflight` too. Header and footer text is no longer reported as not yet drawn.
- Page estimates write a page field that has no result (no `separate`) where it ends, as Word does; it showed nothing. They write the section formats `numberInDash` (`- 1 -`) and `decimalZero` (`01`), apply `\* Upper`, `Lower`, `FirstCap` and `Caps` to the number as written (`\* roman \* Upper` gives `I`), and read a switch written against a word (`PAGE\* roman`). A body page number, and a page reference to a body bookmark, show the page of the line holding the field or the bookmark, not its paragraph's first, and bookmark destinations stay on their text when a field before them changes length. A locked field (`w:fldLock`) keeps its saved result, as Word does.
- `pdf::render` draws underlines, strikethrough, highlight and run shading, as Word for Mac 16.115 draws them (measured on Inter and Times New Roman). Single, words-only and the dotted and dashed underlines sit at the face's `post` underline position and thickness. Double, thick, the heavy forms and the waves start 0.065 em below the baseline and take their widths from the face's thickness: double is two lines half as thick, 0.96 pt apart; thick and heavy lines are twice as thick; a wave is the thickness from peak to trough, stroked 0.24 pt (heavy 0.96), 2.4 pt long. Dash and gap lengths are fixed in points (dotted 1.44/1.44, dash 3.84/1.92, long dash 7.92/3.84, dot-dash and dot-dot-dash likewise). The colour is `w:u w:color`, else the text's. A stretch of underlined text gets one line: at its face's metrics when it has one size, else at 0.88 of the largest run's position and 0.86 of its thickness, fitted to Word within 0.25 pt for two sizes; raised and lowered runs take no part. An underlined or struck tab is drawn to its stop and trailing no-break spaces are underlined; trailing ordinary spaces, including those before a line break, are not. Single and double strikethrough sit at each run's `OS/2` strikeout, raised with the run. Highlight and shading fill the line behind the text, highlight over shading. Automatic text and underline colours are white on run or paragraph shading whose BT.601 luma is below 75/255, else black; a highlight never changes them. Line metrics a font gives out of bounds (thicker than 0.2 em, or off the line) fall back to Arial's. `preflight` no longer reports underline, strikethrough, highlight or run shading as not yet drawn.
- `pdf::render(&Document, &PdfOptions)` (experimental) writes a PDF of the document's body text: each line where page estimates place it, each glyph at the x they measured (tabs to their stops, justified lines spread over their spaces, list labels at the indent), in the face that measured it, with its size, colour, raise or lowering, horizontal scale and caps. A face is the document's embedded font or one from `PdfOptions::fonts`. A bold or italic the family lacks is synthesized as Word for Mac does it (measured): bold stroked size/32.3 wide and scaled to 0.98 of its height, italic sheared 87/256 (18.8°) about its own baseline when raised or lowered. A page outside 3–14,400 pt is refused as `unsupported` (`pdf_page_size`). Text extracts as written. It runs `preflight` and `PdfReport::check` first and reads the document only. A family and style with no font program is refused with `pdf_font_program_missing`, every one listed in the error's new `details.missing_fonts` (`MissingFont`); a character its face has no glyph for, with `pdf_glyph_missing`. `PdfOptions::limits` caps the pages (`resource_limit` `max_pdf_pages`) and the file size, checked while writing. The output is deterministic and reads no clock; `/Info` comes from the core properties. `PdfReport::pages` gives the page count.
- Page estimates give paragraph borders room beside the text, as Word does: a top border's width and space above the first line, a bottom border's below the last. Consecutive paragraphs with equal borders and indents form one group, with the top border above its first paragraph, `between` above each of the others and the bottom border below its last. A `between` line takes its width and its space twice, above and below it, as Word for Mac does. A double line counts three times its width, a triple five. The converter's horizontal rule now takes 1.75 pt more, reference.docx's Title 5 pt and sans-teal's Heading 1 1 pt. 633 paragraph positions moved in 47 layout snapshots, none to another page, and the corpus agreement with Word is unchanged (2,964 of 2,967 paragraphs on Word's page).
- `pdf::render` draws tables, and the preflight no longer refuses them. Each row part on a page fills its cells behind the text (a vertical merge's fill continues on every page it reaches) and draws the borders layout resolved, each maximal run once, as Word for Mac draws them: positions snap to its 1/300-inch grid, a line is drawn in whole 1/300ths (a 1 pt line is 0.96 pt) below its grid line, right of it, or above the table's bottom, and a double or triple line's lines and gaps are each that thick; dotted and dashed lines take paragraph borders' dashes. Where a page break cuts a row, the table's bottom border closes the page and its top border opens the next. Repeated header rows, spans, merges, row exceptions (`w:tblPrEx`) and nested tables are drawn, and tables in headers and footers too. A border style drawn as a single line is reported as `pdf_decoration_approximated` ("table border"), and so is cell or table shading in a pattern other than clear or solid, which is drawn as one colour ("table shading"); diagonal cell borders (`w:tl2br`, `w:tr2bl`) and a paragraph's border or shading inside a table cell are not drawn yet (`pdf_not_yet_drawn`). Table fills and lines count toward the content cap as they are written.
- `pdf::render` draws paragraph borders and shading. Positions and widths follow Word for Mac's PDFs: the box sits 1.44 pt outside the indents and outside the side borders' width and space; a line is drawn in whole 1/300ths of an inch, rounded down (a ¾ pt rule is 0.72 pt), with its inner edge where its width puts it. Side lines run through the spacing the paragraph shares with its neighbours. A fill covers the box inside its lines, behind the text; beside side lines alone it starts 1.44 pt outside the text and leaves the border's space unfilled, as Word does. It runs through the gap to the next paragraph of its group when that one is shaded too. Single, thick, double, triple, dotted, dashed, dash-small-gap, dot-dash and dot-dot-dash lines are drawn as such, with Word's dashes in drawn widths: dashed 4 on and 4 off, dot-dash 7, 3, 3, 3, dot-dot-dash 6 then 2 five times. Any other style is drawn as a single line, and `preflight` reports it as `pdf_decoration_approximated`. `auto` is black. A group split across pages is open at the break. `preflight` no longer reports paragraph borders and shading as not yet drawn; the bar border (`w:bar`) still is.
- `preflight` no longer reports text, tabs, line and page breaks, and numbered lists as not yet drawn. It now reports, as `pdf_not_yet_drawn`, underline, strikethrough, symbols, no-break and soft hyphens (`w:softHyphen` or U+00AD), positional tabs, section breaks, and headers and footers with text, and distributed, kashida and Thai justification as `pdf_justification_unsupported` (drawn as `both`).
- Page estimates measure a variable font at the instance a PDF embeds, in every build (the `skrifa` crate is now a dependency; the wasm grows by about 36 KB), by the rule Word for Mac lays out with (measured on Inter, Inter Italic and Fraunces, 4–24 pt): weight 400, or 700 for bold, italic on the `ital` or `slnt` axis, and every other axis, the optical size included, at its default. Bold Inter headings were measured at the regular instance's advances; no line or page moved. A variable font embedded in the document is still measured at its default instance.
- Page estimates add 0.018 em to each glyph of a bold run set in a face that is not bold, as Word for Mac does when it synthesizes bold (Liberation Serif at 11 pt: 392.64 pt for a line Word sets at 392.79, 389.47 before). No layout snapshot moved.
- `pdf::render` draws tab leaders (dot, hyphen, underscore, heavy as the plain underscore, middle dot) in the tab's font, each character at a whole multiple of its advance from the page's left edge and every one that fits between the tab's ends, as Word for Mac sets them (measured: 309 of 310 leaders in 62 of its PDFs have the same first dot and count). Bar tabs are stroked 0.24 pt wide at their position, from each line's top to its bottom and on the paragraph's last line through its space after, as Word for Mac draws them, and are no longer tab stops. A list label and its suffix space, measured at the label's size, are aligned on the first-line indent by `w:lvlJc` (left, centre or right). A justified line that ends in a line break is spread, as in Word, unless the document sets `w:doNotExpandShiftReturn`. `layout --lines` lists each tab's leader. A leader's font needs a program like the text's. The pages' content before compression is capped at 16 times `max_output_bytes` (`max_pdf_content_bytes`).
- Page estimates end the text after a right tab whose stop is past the right margin at the margin, on the same line, as Word for Mac does; it wrapped to the next line. Small capitals, and the spaces between them, are measured and drawn at 0.8 of the size rounded to a half point, as Word for Mac sets them (9 pt at 11 pt; 8.8 before). No layout snapshot moved.
- `preflight` reports TOC and PAGEREF fields as `pdf_field_cached` instead of not yet drawn: their saved results are drawn as text until the PDF computes its own page numbers. It reports `w:mirrorIndents` as `pdf_layout_feature_ignored`.
- `package::save::destination` gives the file `write_atomic` replaces for a path: the end of its symbolic link chain, which need not exist.
- A list numbering instance restarts the levels it overrides (`w:startOverride`) on first use, so a second converted numbered list starts at 1 instead of continuing the first.
- `pdf::preflight` (experimental) reports what a PDF of a document would leave out or change, before anything is drawn: the body, the headers and footers some page shows, footnotes and endnotes. Paragraph, run and table formatting is resolved as page estimates resolve it (document defaults, table, paragraph and character styles with their `basedOn` chains, the numbering level, direct formatting), so a border, shading, list, page break, frame or right-to-left setting that comes from a style is reported. Each `PdfWarning` has a stable code (`pdf::WARNING_CODES`), a subject, a count and its first ten locations. Constructs the engine does not draw yet are `pdf_not_yet_drawn`, which `PdfReport::check` always refuses (`unsupported_structure`); with `strict`, any warning is refused as `pdf_not_faithful`. `PdfReport::redact` leaves only codes, subjects, counts and locations whose story is a kind (`body`, `header`, `footer`, `footnotes`, `endnotes`), not a part name.
- An error can carry `details` (`Error::details`, `error.details` in its JSON): the warning codes behind it with their counts and fixed descriptions, also listed by `redacted_message` (`; pdf_chart_skipped x1: …`). Errors without details keep their JSON as before.
- The `pdf` feature embeds fonts (internal; no public API yet): each face a document draws with is written as a subset with `subsetter`, a variable font as the instance its weight and italic select, with a `ToUnicode` CMap so text extracts as written, a space and a no-break space sharing a glyph included. A face must read as layout reads fonts. A font whose `fsType` forbids embedding, or allows only bitmaps, is refused with `pdf_font_embedding_restricted`, and one without outlines with `pdf_font_no_outlines`. One that forbids subsetting is embedded whole, without a subset tag; a whole CID-keyed CFF font is refused with `pdf_font_embedding_unsupported`, and a collection face whose tables overlap with `font_malformed`. A variation axis whose range does not hold its default is ignored.
- The `pdf` feature writes links, named destinations and an outline (internal; no public API yet). The painter reports each part of a link with the link's occurrence and its line; touching parts of one link on one line make one `/Link` annotation without a border, and a link that wraps makes one per line. A URI link is written only for `http`, `https`, `mailto` and `tel`, as 7-bit ASCII with other bytes percent-encoded; any other scheme, or a relative target, is left out with `pdf_link_scheme_skipped`, and no Launch, JavaScript or file action is ever written. A link to a bookmark goes to the bookmark's named destination, `[page /XYZ x top 0]` with x 2.16 pt left of the line's text and top the line's top, as Word's PDF has it. Destination names are the bookmark names with `%`, controls and non-ASCII bytes percent-encoded, so every reader sorts the `/Dests` name tree alike. Bookmark names match regardless of case, as Word follows them, and the first of a name wins. `_top` (Word's "Top of the Document") goes to the top of page 1's text area without a bookmark. A link to a bookmark the document does not have is left out with the new `pdf_link_target_missing`; Word's own PDF writes a link that goes nowhere. Each warning counts a link once, however many parts it has and on however many pages a repeated header row draws it; a skipped or missing-target link on a picture or shape is counted as `picture link`, apart from text links. Headings make an open outline (`/PageMode /UseOutlines`), a heading nested under the nearest shallower one whatever levels it skips, with titles in UTF-16 when not ASCII.
- `pdf::preflight` reports a link to a bookmark that no story of the document sets as `pdf_link_target_missing`, once per link; `_top` needs no bookmark.
- A hostile font file, passed with `--font` or embedded in a document, no longer exhausts memory or time. A face is refused when its cmap ranges are out of order or out of range, its cmap subtable overruns its declared length (or, in format 4, lacks the final 0xFFFF segment), or its `hmtx` is missing or short. A collection skips that face, and the new `Face::try_parse` returns `invalid_argument` with reason `font_cmap_invalid`, `font_malformed` or `font_too_large`. The cmap entries and kern records read across one font file, or across one document's embedded fonts, share a budget of 4 × 1,114,112 that is charged before allocating. A font part embedded many times is copied at most once and parsed once per font key, whatever the key's spelling. A family whose embedded font is left unread by the budget is reported in `missing_fonts`, with a `font_metrics_missing` warning, instead of being dropped silently.
- A kern pair listed twice in a font's `kern` table takes its first value, as Word does. Perpetua Titling MT Bold lists period then double quote at −78 and −51 units; estimates measured from the font file used −51, and Word for Mac sets −78.
- Page estimates look font families up by name instead of scanning every face for each new family. A document naming 20,000 families took 0.64 s to lay out, and now takes 0.08 s, with the same result.
- Page estimates clamp out-of-range numbers to Word's bounds instead of using them: lengths to ±22 inches, font sizes to 1–1,638 points, character scale to 1–600 %, line spacing to 132 lines or 22 inches. Infinite and not-a-number values count as absent. A 30-inch page is laid out as a 22-inch one.
- Tables nested deeper than 32 levels are laid out as plain paragraphs of the cell around them, so headings and bookmarks inside them keep a page number.
- A cell's `gridSpan` is clamped to 1–63 columns. A huge value no longer overflows.
- Page estimates have a fixed bound on layout work: 200 million units (pieces, lines, rows and lines drawn, headers and footers included), about 4 seconds. `layout::layout` and `docrs layout` fail with `resource_limit` `max_layout_work` past it. A header or footer shared by many sections is measured once per page width.
- When the contents page numbers of a conversion would pass that bound, the conversion still succeeds: the numbers are not estimated, Word is asked to update fields when it opens the document, and a `toc_page_numbers_skipped` warning says so.
- In justified paragraphs, spaces between a list label or tab and the first word after it no longer let the line shrink to fit; only spaces between words do.
- A table's header rows that are taller than the page body are no longer repeated on the following pages, as in Word. They start on a page of their own and run off its bottom, and the next row starts a new page. A 70-line header over 40 rows was estimated at 43 pages; Word and the estimate now give 3.
- Page estimates align decimal tabs on the decimal point: the text before the first `.` ends at the stop.
- `layout::FontSet` holds font files the caller supplies (TTF, OTF, TTC and OTC; the first 64 faces of a collection), built with `FontSet::builder()`. `add` reads every face of a file and returns each one's family and style as a document names it, whether it is variable or has CFF outlines, and whether its `fsType` allows embedding and subsetting. A file with a face that cannot be read adds nothing and is refused with `invalid_argument`: `font_format_unsupported` for WOFF, WOFF2, Type 1, CFF2, bitmap-only fonts and fonts without a format 4 or 12 cmap, else `font_malformed`, `font_cmap_invalid` or `font_too_large`. All files of a set share one parse budget, and a refused file spends it too. Families are found by name, full name and PostScript name. `alias` lays one family out in another, and an alias to a family found nowhere is ignored. Variable fonts are measured at their default instance.
- `layout::layout_with` lays out with a `FontSet`: each family and style is measured from the document's embedded fonts, then the set, then the bundled table, then a substitute. A style the document does not embed is taken from the set. `layout::layout` keeps its signature and gives the same result.
- `markdown::insert_source_with` numbers the contents with a `FontSet`. `insert_source` and the other conversions behave as before.
- A face read from a font file keeps its glyph for each character and the file it came from (`Face::glyph`, `Face::file`), for the PDF writer to subset later. A face embedded in a document refers to its part, and `FontFile::read` reads it back from the document. Font names are also read from Unicode-platform name records, so Lucida Grande's collection reads all four faces.
- Page estimates treat strike and hidden as toggle properties, like bold, italic, caps and small caps: they XOR across the table, paragraph and character styles. Within one style's `basedOn` chain the nearest value wins, as Word does: a style based on a bold style with bold off is regular. No layout snapshot changed.
- The layout model carries each run's look for the PDF painter: size and rise, colour (theme colours resolved with their tint and shade), bold, italic, underline style and colour, strike, highlight, shading, caps, spacing, scale and hyperlink target, with paragraph shading and borders and cell fill, vertical alignment and borders as read. Lines and pages are unchanged.
- The `pdf` feature reads images into image XObjects (internal; no public API yet), recognising the format by its bytes. JPEG is passed through: grey, RGB, progressive, and Adobe CMYK with its inverted values. PNG is passed through when it is 8-bit grey, RGB or palette without transparency or interlace, and decoded otherwise: every colour type and depth (16 bits reduced to 8), Adam7, and palette or `tRNS` transparency as a soft mask. GIF is decoded to its first frame with its transparent colour, and BMP when uncompressed (1, 4, 8, 24 and 32 bits, either row order). Each media part is one XObject. An embedded colour profile warns `pdf_image_profile_ignored`, an animation `pdf_image_animated`; TIFF, EMF, WMF, SVG, YCCK JPEG, 12-bit or arithmetic-coded JPEG and compressed or 16-bit BMP are refused with `pdf_image_unsupported`, and damaged data with `pdf_image_malformed`: a JPEG without scan data, a passed-through PNG whose compressed data runs past its last row or holds an index outside its palette (bytes after its zlib stream are left out of the PDF), a PNG critical chunk that fails its CRC (a damaged ancillary chunk is skipped, as libpng does), a BMP whose colour masks are not separate runs of bits.
- `pdf::PdfLimits` (experimental) holds the PDF export's budgets: pages (2,000; 500 for `serverless()`), output bytes (256 MiB; 32 MiB), decoded pixels per image (32 million; 4 million) and per document (256 million; 32 million); `trusted()` lifts all but a 4 GiB file. Image sizes are checked from the header, before decoding, as `resource_limit` `max_image_pixels` or `max_document_pixels`; memory then grows with the data, not with the header. A PNG is decoded row by row, its colour and alpha planes growing with the rows read; a row of more than 4 MiB is refused with `pdf_image_unsupported`. GIF decoding stops at the frame's last pixel. A part that fails is read once, and its pixels count toward the document. Memory that cannot be reserved is `resource_limit` `out_of_memory`.
- Page estimates lay out each page's header and footer instead of reserving the tallest one's height: the first page's with `w:titlePg`, an even page's with `w:evenAndOddHeaders`, else the default, each inherited from the section before when a section names none. A header or footer taller than its margin pushes the body down or up as before. As in Word for Mac, a header draws only the lines whose top is less than half the page less 10 points below the header distance, whatever the margins, and the body starts below the last one's space after; a footer is cut the same way from its top, and also where it would leave the body less than 36 points below the header. A cut story gives an internal `pdf_story_overflow` warning for the PDF report. No layout snapshot changed.
- Page estimates compute page fields: `PAGE` (arabic, `\* roman`, `ROMAN`, `alphabetic`, `ALPHABETIC`, else the section's `w:pgNumType w:fmt`), `NUMPAGES`, `SECTIONPAGES` and `PAGEREF` (`\p` is ignored with `pdf_field_switch_ignored`), and `TITLE`, `AUTHOR`, `SUBJECT`, `KEYWORDS` and `DOCPROPERTY` from the document's properties. Number formats layout does not write (`\* Ordinal`, `CardText` and the like, `\#` pictures) are shown in arabic with `pdf_field_format_approximated`; letters past 30 repeats (780) are written in arabic, and `w:pgNumType w:start` is kept within 0 and 2,147,483,647, so a huge start no longer exhausts memory. Custom properties show as written only when they are text or numbers. Other fields show their stored result; `DATE`, `TIME` and `PRINTDATE` keep it with `pdf_field_cached`. Nothing is fetched. `layout::layout` and `layout_with` show the stored results of page counts and references and compute each header's and footer's page number.
- Contents page numbers come from one settled layout: the layout is repeated, each pass showing the page numbers, counts and references the pass before gave, until a pass gives the same values and breaks as the one before. Values that repeat an earlier pass, or still change after 8 passes, are refused with `pdf_layout_not_converged`; a conversion, and the Node package's renumbering after `fields` are filled, then keep the stored numbers, ask Word to update them and warn `toc_page_numbers_skipped`, as past the work bound. Each number is written as the settled layout shows it, in its section's format: a contents in a lower-roman section reads ii, iii, where it was written 2, 3. On the 84 corpus conversions and the contents fixtures, every entry shows `Layout::bookmark_page`'s page and the number the layout draws.
- Superscript is drawn raised 0.41 em and subscript lowered 0.087 em, as Word for Mac draws them at 11 pt (they were 1/3 and 0.08 em). A `pctN` shading moves each channel toward the pattern colour by its share rounded half up, as Word does: `pct50` black on white is 7F7F7F, not 808080.
- Superscript and subscript are the font's OS/2 superscript or subscript size, rounded to the nearest half point, as Word for Mac draws them: Arial is 7 pt at 11 pt and 8 pt at 12 pt, and Inter is 6.5 pt at 11 pt (they were 2/3). A face from the bundled metrics, or one whose OS/2 table has no usable value, uses 1331/2048 em, the size every measured Microsoft font has. Lines with them are slightly narrower; no line breaks differently and no layout snapshot changed.
- A table row taller than what is left of the page splits page after page, not once: each cell continues from where it was cut, and a row at the top of a page splits too (it used to run off the bottom). A line no page has room for starts a page and runs off its bottom; one that only this page's headers or footers leave too little room for waits for a page with room, unless no other cell can place a line, so a split always ends. A nested table splits with the cell holding it instead of moving whole. Repeated header rows are drawn on every page a split row continues on. Can't-split rows still move whole. A row whose lines fit but whose space after or bottom margin does not moves whole to the next page, as Word does (it stayed and the next row started a page). A header or can't-split row cut at the paper draws every line whose top is above the edge, not only those whose baseline is. No layout snapshot changed; a 60-level nested table's last levels now land on page 2.

A table on a style of its own takes only that style's chain, so it has none when the chain sets none. A document with no default table style has none. Every converted document carries Table Normal with 5.4 points, so its line breaks are unchanged.
- Below compatibility mode 15 (Word 2010 and older, or no settings part or mode), a table that is not nested in another starts left of the margin by its first cell's left margin, so its text lines up with the body's, as Word draws it. `w:tblInd` moves it too. Only x changes: in the PDF fixture `table-times-new-roman` (mode 14), "Region" starts at 90.0 as in Word, not 95.4.
- `markdown::inspect` (`markdown-inspect`) reports a template whose settings name no compatibility mode as Word 2007 mode (`old_compatibility_mode`), as Word lays it out; it reported Word 2013 mode. Its message says that a conversion replacing the body writes Word 2013 mode.
- Page estimates lay out an equation (`m:oMath`) as one line of text after Word's linear format (`(n+1)/2`, `√(x+1)`, `∑_(i=1)^n i`, `[■(a&b@c&d)]`), in its place in the paragraph; it was measured as its bare text at the end of the paragraph. Subscripts and superscripts are set as Word sets them, by Cambria Math's MATH table (73 % size, 60 % within a script, raised 750 and lowered 418 of 2048 units, 85 units of space after), whatever font draws the equation; a supplied Cambria Math's own MATH table is read instead, without their `_` and `^` and without brackets that only group, and a raised script makes its line taller: `a^2+b^2=c^2` is drawn as `a2 + b2 = c2` with each 2 raised. Binary operators take a medium math space on each side and relations a thick one, as Word sets them, except in scripts, limits and `m:nor` text. A display equation (`m:oMathPara`, or an `m:oMath` alone in its paragraph) is a line of its own, centred unless its `m:jc` says left or right, and as tall as Word stacks it: a fraction two text lines, a matrix or equation array one per row, an n-ary operator or a limit one plus its limits at script size, nested stacks adding; past 16 lines, one line. Letters without an `m:sty` are slanted, as Word draws them. An equation is set in Cambria Math only when the document embeds it or the caller supplies it, else in its paragraph's font, so an equation never needs a font its paragraph does not. The layout model keeps each equation's linear text, with `^` and `_`, for a PDF's `ActualText`. `pdf::preflight` quotes it in each `pdf_equation_linearized` warning, flags an accent as combining marks (`pdf_text_unsupported`) and skips equations in tracked deletions. An `m:chr`, `m:begChr`, `m:endChr` or `m:sepChr` counts its first character only. In `showcase.md`, the two display equations move down: 1.1 and 19.4 points in the reference template.
- Page estimates lay out footnotes and endnotes. A line referring to footnotes needs them at the foot of its page, under the separator note (laid out as its paragraph, spacing included; below compatibility mode 15 its spacing counts only above a note going on), on the body's bottom edge, the citing line keeping its paragraph's space after above them; when they don't fit, the line moves to the next page with them, as in Word. A line's last note may instead start on its page and go on under the continuation separator when one of its lines fits, and goes on under the continuation separator's line alone, with no spacing; a note longer than a page runs over as many pages as it needs, each line once. Word for Mac's PDF of a 69-paragraph note puts its lines where the estimate does (53 on page 1 from 178.08, the rest of page 2 from 98.40). On a page with notes a line's full height must clear them, multiple spacing's extra included. A reference in a table cell reserves on the page its row, or the part of a split row holding it, lands: a row splitting over pages gives up its deepest lines on a page until the notes of the lines it keeps fit, and ends at the line citing a note that goes on, as Word does. Endnotes follow the last block under their separator. Marks are numbered by `w:footnotePr` and `w:endnotePr` (format, start, restart per section or page) in the settings and each section, or are the text after `w:customMarkFollows`, and are measured in the Footnote Reference style. A reference used to be measured as a hyphen and its note took no room, so pages held more text than Word's. On the 93 Word oracle documents (`pr-proof/corpus`), 2,964 of 2,967 paragraphs start on Word's page instead of 2,840, and all 93 have Word's page count instead of 85; the 21 conversions of `showcase.md` and `examples/markdown/reference.docx` are the layout snapshots that moved.
- `docrs layout --lines` lists note lines with story `footnotes` or `endnotes`, the separators as empty lines, and each mark as its number.
- The `pdf` painter draws a note's mark as `layout --lines` places it, its digits spread over the width layout measured, and refuses with `pdf_footnote_layout_unsupported` a note whose table would go on to the next page. Separator rules are not drawn yet, so preflight still reports notes as `pdf_not_yet_drawn`.
- `pdf::preflight` refuses (`pdf_footnote_layout_unsupported`, also without `strict`) footnotes placed beneath the text, footnote columns (`w15:footnoteColumns`), endnotes at the end of each of several sections, and notes referenced only from another note, which the layout does not place as Word does.
- Page estimates lay out each section as Word does. A next-page break starts a new page. An odd- or even-page break first adds a blank page when the number the next page would show has the wrong parity; Word goes by the shown number, not the physical page. The blank page has the next section's paper, shows no header or footer, counts in `PAGE` and `NUMPAGES` but in no section's `SECTIONPAGES`, as in Word. A continuous break stays on the page when both sections use the same paper size, taking the new left and right margins at once and the top and bottom ones from the next page; between different paper sizes it starts a new page, as Word does. A column break starts a page, since one column is laid out. A section's `w:vAlign` `center` or `bottom` moves each of its pages' body down by half or all of the room left below it, above the page's footnotes, which stay at the foot; an empty section-break paragraph ending the page takes no room, so the cover of `cover-page-sections` has its title's baseline at 504.2 points, where Word has 504.7 (it was 300.4). `both` is laid out at the top, and preflight reports it as `pdf_layout_feature_ignored` (`vertical page justification`); `center` and `bottom` are no longer reported. The `cover-page-sections` template's cover moves down by 203.8 points; the corpus does not change. `Layout::page_label` gives the number a page shows with its section's restarts and format (`iii`, `1`); `Layout::page_number` stays as it was.
- A justified line that starts or ends with a footnote mark standing as a word widens every space between its words; it left the first or last space unwidened, one gap short of what the line breaker counted.
- Tables take their style's conditional formats as Word for Mac does: the whole table, then horizontal and vertical bands (column bands show over row bands), first and last column, first and last row, then the corners, as `w:tblLook` turns them on, with the style's band sizes. When `w:tblLook` has any of its attributes, its `w:val` bitmask is ignored and each attribute left out is on (`firstRow`, `lastRow`, `firstColumn`, `lastColumn`, `noHBand`, `noVBand`); with none, the bitmask decides; without `w:tblLook`, `04A0`. Bands skip a shown header row and first column. Every leading row marked `w:tblHeader` takes the first row's format, as Word for Mac draws it, so a header of several rows is one first row and bands start below it; the converted "Passenger Traffic" table's five header rows are all the header's colour, and 17 corpus layout snapshots moved (its first body row is within 0.01 pt of Word's top in `us-letter`, 14 pt lower than before). Each format's paragraph and run properties reach the cell's text, so a header row's bold or a last row's size changes lines; its fill, vertical alignment and borders reach the cell. `cnfStyle` is ignored: the cell's position decides. Row exceptions (`w:tblPrEx`) give their row table borders and cell margins; Word draws no fill from their `w:shd`, and neither do page estimates.
- Without `overrideTableStyleFontSizeAndJustification`, an 11 or 12 point size or left alignment that a cell's paragraph has from the default paragraph style gives way to the table style's, as in Word 2007 documents; with it, the standard order holds.
- Cells sit on the table grid (`gridBefore` and `gridSpan` included), and a table centred or right-aligned (`w:jc`) sits so in its column; such a table is not outdented.
- Table borders are resolved per cell and edge: the cell's own, then the conditional formats', the row exception's and the table's, where `none` defers to the next level and `nil` ends the lookup with no line. Adjacent cells' edges are weighed by MS-OI29500's ranking, as Word for Mac draws it: `none` and `nil` both yield to the other cell's line, then the heavier border wins (its drawn width in eighths of a point times the style's number, a double counting its two lines and the gap between them; dotted and dashed weigh 1), then the earlier style, then the darker colour. A double 0.5 point border beats a single 1.5 point one. Merged cells have no edges inside them. Each row's height adds the widest resolved border above it (and below the last row), so a header row's conditional bottom border now counts: in `pdf-table-times-new-roman` the step from the header row to the first body row is 13.64 points (it was 13.14; Word's is 13.68).
- A vertically merged cell keeps its first cell's fill and alignment in every row it covers, across pages too. Cells align their content to the top, centre or bottom of their row; a row split across pages aligns only its last part.
- Word reads no whole-table cell borders from a table style (MS-OI29500 §2.1.255), and neither do page estimates now.
- `pdf::preflight` reports table cell spacing (`pdf_layout_feature_ignored`, "cell spacing").
- `pdf::render` draws each character through the font chain: the face that measured it when that face has the glyph in a program it may embed; a character the face lacks in the next face that has it (a supplied face of the family, its alias, then the declared fallbacks), which also measures it, so its ascent and descent join the line height. `pdf_glyph_missing` is a warning when a fallback draws the character, and a refusal only when nothing can. For a PDF, a family no other source provides is drawn and measured in the caller's declared fallback for its kind (`FontSetBuilder::fallback(FontKind, family)`, reported as `pdf_font_substituted`); without one it is refused as before. Word's layout, which numbers a .docx's contents, ignores declared fallbacks, so a font set shared by a conversion and an export never changes a .docx's page numbers; `layout::LayoutTarget` names the two, and `layout::records_for` lays out for either. A family's own supplied face comes before its alias. Footnote and endnote text and every character their marks can show are decided and refused with the body.
- `FontSetBuilder::metric_aliases` (off by default) lays Arial out in Liberation Sans, Times New Roman and Times in Liberation Serif, and Courier New in Liberation Mono, when those fonts are supplied: their advances equal those of Word's fonts over every code point both map, except U+2010 HYPHEN (tested), so a PDF drawn through them breaks lines as Word does and is not reported; each U+2010 drawn through Arial's or Times New Roman's alias is (`pdf_font_substituted`). An alias set with `alias` is reported as `pdf_font_substituted`, unless it is one of those tested pairs, which is treated as `metric_aliases` treats it (so the Node package's `loadFonts` `aliases` option mapping Times New Roman to Liberation Serif is silent too).
- A bold or italic drawn from another style of a family whose Word font has that style (the bundled table) is reported as `pdf_font_substituted`; a family without such a style is still synthesized silently, as Word does.
- A variable font also answers to the name Word for Mac lists it under: its family and the `STAT` label of its default optical size, unless the label is elidable ("Fraunces 9pt", "Literata 12pt"; Inter stays "Inter"). Documents saved by Word for Mac name such fonts that way, and plain-family names keep working. `FontInfo::word_family` gives the name. Word itself substitutes a font for a document naming plain "Fraunces"; a PDF draws Fraunces.
- For a PDF, a face whose `fsType` forbids embedding, or allows only bitmaps, counts as missing: the next face of the chain measures and draws its text, and `pdf_font_substituted` names it ("its licence forbids embedding"), as it does a style drawn from another of the family because its own face is restricted; with none, it is refused as a missing program, the message saying why. Word's layout still measures with it.
- `PdfOptions::allow_unembedded` references a face with no program it may embed by name, as a TrueType font in `WinAnsiEncoding` whose `/Widths` are the measured advances and whose descriptor takes the face's own metrics where its program can be read (else estimates from the measured advances and line metrics), instead of refusing (`pdf_font_not_embedded`).
- Symbols: Symbol and Wingdings codes are mapped to the Unicode characters they stand for. The converter's bullets (Symbol's •, Wingdings' ▪, Courier New's `o` in a list label) and ballot boxes (☐ ☑ ☒ in MS Gothic) are drawn from a built-in Type 3 font (another one for every 255 distinct symbols), without a warning, so a strict PDF of a bulleted or task list succeeds; they extract as •, ▪, o and the boxes. Another symbol drawn without its font is drawn as what it stands for by a declared fallback, else as a box, and reported as `pdf_symbol_substituted`. Symbol fonts never fall back to a text font for their codes.
- `w:sym` is measured and drawn as its `w:char` in its `w:font`, and `preflight` no longer reports it as not yet drawn.
- `PdfReport::fonts` lists each family and style the document asks for with what drew it (`PdfFontUse`: `document`, `supplied`, `alias`, `fallback`, `glyph_fallback`, `symbols`, `symbol_fallback` or `not_embedded`) and whether its bold or italic was synthesized; `redact` replaces family names the bundled table does not hold.
- `pdf::used_fonts(&Document)` lists the families and styles the body text is set in, whether the document embeds each, and whether the built-in symbol font draws all of it, for loading only the fonts a PDF needs.

### Node package

- `loadFonts(files, options?)` and `loadFontManifest(entries, options?)` (experimental) load font files once as a `FontSet` handle, which `fromHtml`, `fromJson`, `fromMarkdown` and `fromTiptap` take as `fonts` to measure the contents page numbers with. A file that cannot be read refuses the whole set and is named by `error.file`. The handle keeps a copy of its files and is loaded again into a fresh instance after a panic; `free()` releases it, and a freed handle throws `pdf_font_handle_disposed`. Budgets per `limits` (one file, the set, faces, live handles, bytes of live handles: 32 MiB, 64 MiB, 64, 64, 4 GiB by default; 8 MiB, 16 MiB, 24, 8, 128 MiB with `serverless`) are checked on sizes before any copy and again by each call under its own `limits`; live handles are counted across instances. ### Command line

- `docrs pdf` and the convert commands with `--format pdf` take `--no-outline` and `--no-links` (experimental): the PDF has no outline, or no clickable links and bookmark destinations.
- `docrs pdf IN.docx --out OUT.pdf [--font FILE]… [--font-dir DIR]… [--strict]` writes a PDF (experimental; no longer hidden); `--preflight` still prints the report alone. The file is written atomically, and an output that is the input, through a symbolic or hard link (on every platform) or a directory the write would create (`new/../in.docx`, or a dangling symbolic link through one), is refused (`output_is_input`, exit 2), and a refusal creates nothing. A missing font program exits 3 with the families in `error.details.missing_fonts`. JSON: `docrs_pdf` with the report under `pdf`.
- `from-markdown`, `from-html` and `from-json` take `--format docx|pdf` (default `docx`; never taken from the output's extension). With `pdf`, the converted document is written as `docrs pdf` writes it, `--strict` refuses a PDF warning too, and the JSON report has `pdf` instead of `save`.
- `docrs layout` shows each paragraph's text as Word displays it (field results, tabs, line breaks, non-breaking hyphens and symbols, not field codes), and marks the paragraphs of a table of contents with `toc`.
- `layout --lines` prints every line of the estimated layout as it is laid out, one JSON object a line: `page`, `story`, `x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `baseline`, `tabs`, `slack`, `gaps`, `last`, `align` and `text` (experimental).
- `layout --lines` prints each page's header and footer lines before its body's, with `story` `headerN` or `footerN`, and shows page fields with their settled values.
- `from-markdown`, `from-html`, `from-json` and `layout` take `--font FILE` and `--font-dir DIR` (both repeatable). The contents page numbers and the layout are measured with those fonts where the document embeds none of the family and style. A bad `--font` file fails the command. `--font-dir` reads the `.ttf`, `.otf`, `.ttc` and `.otc` files of the directory. Files it cannot read, files over 64 MiB (`font_file_too_large`), and files that are not supported fonts are skipped and listed in `fonts_skipped` with their reason.
- `docrs fonts FILE… | --font-dir DIR` lists the families and styles font files provide, as a document must name them. It also shows whether each face has CFF outlines, is variable, or forbids embedding or subsetting (`--json` for the `docrs_fonts` report).
- `layout --lines` first prints the document's `looks` and `links`: each run's size, rise, colour (theme colours resolved with their tint and shade), bold, italic, underline style and colour, strike, highlight, shading, caps, spacing, scale and link, and each link's URI or bookmark. Each line then has `runs`, its text split by look (experimental).
- `layout --lines` baselines sit where Word for Mac draws them. With auto or multiple line spacing, the baseline is the line's largest ascent (line gap included) below its top, and the extra space goes below the text, 1.8 to 2.5 pt higher than before with 1.15 spacing. With exact spacing or a multiple below 1, the baseline is 0.8 of the line height. Line heights, pitch and page breaks are unchanged.
- `docrs pdf --preflight IN.docx` prints the PDF preflight report (JSON with `--json`), with what the package reader set aside (`set_aside`). The command is hidden and experimental, and writes no PDF yet.
- `layout --lines` also prints each part of a table row on a page, as an object with `row` (its index in its table), `page`, `story`, `x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `first` and `last` (the row starts and ends on this page), `repeat` (a header row repeated on a later page) and `cells` (each one's `x`, `width`, and the content offsets `from` and `to` shown on this page) (experimental).
- `layout --lines` gives each line and row part its page's size, `page_width` and `page_height` in points: a landscape section's pages read 792 by 612 on Letter paper.
- `layout --lines` row parts give each cell's `fill` and the row's `edges`: each border line after conflicts, above, below and beside its cells, with its `from` and `to` points, `style`, `width` and `colour`. Where a page break cuts a row, the page before draws the table's bottom border at the cut and the page after its top border, across a vertical merge too, as Word does.
- `docrs pdf`, and the convert commands with `--format pdf`, take `--allow-unembedded`. `--fallback KIND=FAMILY` (`serif`, `sans` or `mono`; repeatable) and `--metric-aliases` join `--font` and `--font-dir`; `--fallback` applies to PDFs only. A malformed `--fallback` exits 2 (`fallback_invalid`), and so does one naming a family no font given provides (`fallback_not_supplied`). `layout --lines --pdf` lays out as `docrs pdf` does.
- `docrs fonts` shows the name Word for Mac gives a variable font (`word_family` in JSON).

## 1.0.0 (2026-11-13)

The first stable release. The library, the command line and the Node.js package follow semantic versioning from here on. Nothing is removed: `fromTiptap` and `from-markdown --from` still work.

### Library

- A header or footer left behind by `set_linked_to_previous(true)` is no longer searched or edited. `find_text` and `replace_all` went on finding text in it until the document was saved and reopened. Which parts the document reaches is now worked out again whenever an edit adds or removes a part or changes a relationship.
- Page estimates no longer count a nested field's result as visible text while the field around it is still in its code. `{ IF { MERGEFIELD … } … }` measured the inner result, which moved the page numbers a table of contents estimates.
- A role that fails no longer hides another role whose name starts the same way. A failed `table_caption` made `markdown-styles` report `table` missing with Table Grid present, and a failed `definition_term` did the same to `definition`.
- Every table of contents in one document gets page numbers and the front matter's `toc-depth`. With front-matter `toc` and a `[TOC]` both present, the first contents kept "1" for every page, and the `[TOC]` one ignored `toc-depth` and did not ask Word to update fields.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-22)

### Library

- **Package and XML:**
  - A removed part's name can be used again, in the same edit or a later one. Before, adding a part under a removed name failed with the internal error `duplicate_part`. A part re-added with identical bytes saves as the original. Handles into the removed member (spans, blocks, paragraphs, runs, tables, controls) are stale against the new one (`story_replaced`, `stale_handle`, …) even when its content lines up (`XmlTree::incarnation`).
  - Opening a package is linear in its part count: 64k parts open in about 0.3 s instead of 48 s.
  - Copying deeply nested markup (deep copies, importing from another document as `append` and `insert-from` do) and comparing run properties during replace no longer recurse. With `--trusted`, 40k levels of nested content controls no longer abort `append` with a stack overflow.
- **Protection:** every inherited writer now honours Restrict Editing: authoring (paragraphs, runs, tables, cells, pictures, tab stops, `clear_body_content`), sections, headers and footers, styles and latent styles, and core properties. A forbidden edit refuses `document_protected` and changes nothing; `Document::acknowledge_protection` lets it through. `Run::mark_comment_range` counts as a comment edit. `Document::ensure_styles_part` refuses only when it would create the part.
- **Compare (breaking for struct literals of `CompareOptions`):** an original under Restrict Editing that forbids body edits is refused unless the new `CompareOptions::acknowledge_protection` is set (CLI: `compare --acknowledge-protection`). The redline no longer comes back with protection acknowledged on the caller's behalf. `author` is validated like other writers' text (XML 1.0, no control characters, at most `MAX_AUTHOR_CHARS` = 255 characters), and the redline is committed through the transaction path. Code that builds `CompareOptions` with a struct literal needs the new field (`acknowledge_protection: false` keeps the safe default).
- **Comments:** a span captured under `view=original` or `view=all` no longer anchors a comment (`Span::comment`, `comments::add_comment`); it refuses `historical_view_target` like replace does, so a comment cannot anchor on deleted text.
- **Comment ids:** allocating a comment `w:id` after an id at the 64-bit limit no longer overflows; past the signed 32-bit range the lowest free id is reused as before. A new `w14:paraId` that would reach `0x80000000` refuses `invalid_argument(para_id_exhausted)` instead of writing an id Word rejects.
- **Revisions:** `Revisions::accept_all` / `reject_all` refresh the caller's snapshot from the staged trees before committing, so they can no longer report an error after the document has changed.
- **Markdown and HTML to Word, hostile input:**
  - Inline HTML closed across Markdown emphasis (`<b>*x</b>*`) no longer panics; the Markdown spans close with the HTML span and reopen after it, so the text and its formatting are kept.
  - An unclosed inline HTML tag ends with its block instead of swallowing what follows (a nested list, display math).
  - Front matter indented with a non-ASCII space (U+00A0) no longer panics: YAML indentation is ASCII spaces only, anything else is content.
  - Structure nested deeper than 128 levels (`markdown::MAX_NESTING_DEPTH`: block quotes, lists, emphasis, links, images, HTML elements, math groups) is refused with `ResourceLimit` (`markdown_nesting_too_deep`) before anything changes, instead of overflowing the stack.
  - Tables are narrowed to Word's 63 columns: spans past the last column are cut back and cells past it merge into it, with a `table_columns_clamped` warning. 300 cells with `colspan="1000"` took tens of seconds and wrote 300,000 columns.
  - HTML browsers do not display (`head`, `title`, `style`, `script`, `template`, `noscript`, …) is dropped with its content and an `html_element_skipped` warning per element kind, instead of becoming body paragraphs. Script, style and title text is no longer read as markup.
- **Markdown and HTML to Word, untrusted input:**
  - Local images are read only when `MarkdownOptions::resource_dir` is set, and only from inside it: absolute paths, drive and UNC prefixes, `file:` URLs and paths leading out of it (`..`, symbolic links) are refused with an `image_outside_resource_dir` warning. **Behaviour change:** with `resource_dir: None` (the default) nothing is read from disk; pass images in `images` or set the directory.
  - Image warnings quote the reference as written, never the resolved path or the operating system's error.
  - Pictures larger than the document's `Limits::max_part_bytes` (`data:` URIs measured before decoding) are left out with an `image_too_large` warning.
  - A damaged or truncated picture is left out with an `image_unreadable` warning instead of failing the conversion; a resolution of zero is taken as 96 dpi (`Image::layout_dimensions`).
  - Links keep their target only for `http`, `https`, `mailto`, `tel`, `#fragment` and relative references; other schemes (`javascript:`, `file:`, ...) keep their text without the link (`link_scheme_skipped`).
  - The template's package thumbnail (a picture of its first page) is left out of replace-body conversions and listed in `removed_parts`.
  - `images` keys and references match alike: `./a.png` finds `a.png`, `a%20b.png` finds `a b.png`, and the other way round.
  - Very deeply nested block quotes no longer overflow the quote depth.
- **Hyperlinks:** `add_hyperlink` refuses `javascript:` and `vbscript:` addresses and addresses with control characters (`invalid_argument`).
- **Images:** `Image::from_vec` characterises owned bytes without copying them.

### Command line

- `compare --acknowledge-protection` redlines an original under Restrict Editing; without it such an original is refused (exit 3).
- `from-markdown` reads images from the current directory for standard input, as before, and only from inside the Markdown file's directory or `--resource-path`.
- **Panics** are reported as an internal error (exit 70, code `internal`, reason `internal_panic`, the JSON envelope under `--json`) instead of Rust's panic message and exit 101.
- **Argument errors under `--json`** are the JSON error envelope (`invalid_argument`, exit 2) instead of plain text. `--help` and `--version` still print text and exit 0.
- **`replace --all`** exits 3 and writes nothing when it replaces nothing (no match: `target_not_found`; every match refused: the first refusal), as `replace` without `--all` does. Reports give `replaced_count` and `refused_count`.
- **`layout --font`** with an unreadable file is an I/O error (exit 5), not an invalid argument.

### Node.js

- **Budgets before copies:** the source, the reference document and images are checked against the `limits` budgets by their size before they are copied into WebAssembly memory. The reference is also held to the package budget `max_archive_bytes` up front.
- **`markdownStyles(reference?, styles?, options?)`** takes options; `limits`, `strictPackage` and `redact` apply as in the other functions.
- **`now`** must be a finite number of milliseconds from 0 to the end of the year 9999; anything else is refused (`invalid_argument`, `invalid_options`).
- **Arguments:** a source that is not a string is refused (`invalid_source`); the `reference` argument of `inspectReference` and `markdownStyles` must be a `Uint8Array` (a `Buffer` is one).
- **Builds** of the npm package use `Cargo.lock` (`--locked`), and CI builds wasm-bindgen-cli from crates.io instead of downloading an unverified binary.
- **Panics** (bugs) print their message on `console.error`. The call still throws `RuntimeError: unreachable`; load the module again before the next call (README).

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-21)

The first release. Three packages:
- `docrs`, the library;
- `docrs-cli`, the `docrs` command;
- `docrs-wasm` on npm, WebAssembly for Node.js.

### Library

- **Behaviour:** checked operation by operation against the Python implementation it adapts, used as a test oracle; the differences are documented in `docs/compatibility.md`.
- **Package and XML:**
  - Guarded package reading: ZIP central-directory validation and resource limits (`Limits`). Irregularities Word tolerates (Office `[trash]` members, data in ZIP directory entries) are refused by default; an opt-in `Tolerance` sets them aside and reports what it set aside.
  - A lossless, namespace-preserving XML arena.
  - Patch saves that rewrite only changed parts. A no-op save is byte-identical.
- **Editing:**
  - Search with normalisation, outlines and stories.
  - Replace one or all occurrences.
  - Tracked changes (make, list, accept, reject) and comments.
  - Comparison into a verified redline.
  - Appending or inserting documents with styles, numbering, media, links and bookmarks reconciled.
  - Numbering, tables, sections, headers and footers, fields, bookmarks, links, footnotes, content controls, document properties and protection.
- **Handles and refusals:** handles carry freshness evidence, and refusals are typed and atomic.
- **Markdown and HTML to Word** (`markdown` feature): Markdown or editor HTML into a reference template.
  - It takes the template's styles, numbering, page setup, headers, footers and the branding its sample content shows.
  - It leaves out the template's sample body, comments and private document properties.
  - Template inspection (`markdown::inspect`), serverless resource presets and redacted warnings.
- **Table of contents page numbers:** computed by estimating Word for Mac's layout from bundled font metrics (numbers only, no font software). Fonts embedded in the template are used when present. On 84 test documents, 95.8% of entries match Word's own update exactly and none is more than one page off.
